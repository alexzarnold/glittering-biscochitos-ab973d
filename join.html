  3. Turn up

    Sit anywhere. Nobody makes you introduce yourself.

Member to analyst

A ladder with a clear first rung.

Selection happens after you have had every chance to prepare, not before you walk in.

  1. Become eligible

    Consistent attendance and engagement, plus good standing. That is the whole entry requirement, and it is in your control.

  2. Apply and interview

    The process may include an application, an interview, and an evaluation of analytical ability. Not your resume, not your major.

  3. Present

    Analysts prepare structured analyses, present them, and take part in peer evaluation. This is the part that makes you better.

If this is your first week, ignore the analyst track. Come to two or three meetings first.

Questions

The things people ask

Do I need experience?

No. The most common answer on our sign-up form is "never invested." Meetings assume nothing. If a word comes up that you do not know, ask.

Is this only for finance majors?

No. Options and risk are applied probability. Math, computer science, engineering, economics, and philosophy students all do well here.

Do you manage real money?

No. ORCA does not run a fund, pool member money, or invest real capital. Nothing we discuss is investment advice.

How much time is this?

About an hour every other week. Analysts spend more, but nobody joins that track by accident.

Can I join mid-semester?

Yes. Meetings do not build on each other. Come to the next one.
