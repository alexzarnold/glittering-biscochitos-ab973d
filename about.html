About

A club about how uncertainty is priced.

Founded 2026 at the University of Colorado Boulder.

Purpose

Why we exist

ORCA is a student-led investment organization for people who want to understand options, risk management, and capital allocation properly.

Four commitments. Build analytical and probabilistic thinking. Judge returns against the risk taken to earn them. Create a structured environment for market analysis and debate. Prepare members for careers in trading, investment management, and risk.

The meeting format, the Analyst Program, and the reading list all exist to serve those four.

How we are different

Most finance clubs pitch stocks. We look at the other side of the trade.

A stock pitch asks one question: will this go up? Useful, and it quietly assumes being right is all that matters.

We start elsewhere. How much do you lose if you are wrong. How often do you need to be right. What is the shape of the payoff and what does it cost to own. How big should the position be.

That is the daily work of a derivatives trader, a risk manager, and a portfolio manager.

Structure

Three tiers, one ladder.

Everyone enters at the same place. Movement upward is by application and work.

General Members

Open to all students

Any enrolled CU Boulder student. No application, no obligation to attend every session.

Analysts

Application and interview

Members in good standing with consistent attendance may be considered. Analysts present structured analyses and take part in peer evaluation.

Executive Board

President, Vice President, Treasurer

Three officers responsible for governance, strategy, and finances. One academic year, reappointment possible.

General meetings are open to every member. Analyst meetings are restricted to analysts and the Executive Board.

Standards

How we expect people to behave.

A club about honest reasoning only works if the room is honest.

  • Professionalism. Show up prepared and on time.
  • Respect. Attack the argument, never the person. Beginners get the same courtesy as everyone else.
  • Intellectual honesty. Show your reasoning. Say plainly when you were wrong.
  • Transparency. Decisions, finances, and selection get explained to the membership.
  • Continuous evaluation. We review our own structure and change what is not working.
